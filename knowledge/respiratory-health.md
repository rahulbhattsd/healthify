# Upper Respiratory Symptoms and Cough Care

Upper respiratory infections, colds, and seasonal allergies frequently cause nasal congestion, sore throat, sneezing, and persistent coughs. Distinguishing between viral congestion and secondary bacterial issues helps guide effective comfort measures.

## Congestion and Sinus Pressure
Swollen nasal passages and mucus accumulation lead to facial pressure and blocked breathing:
- Non-drug measures: Saline nasal sprays or sinus rinses help thin mucus and clear airborne irritants. Inhaling warm steam from a shower or using a cool-mist humidifier in the bedroom keeps respiratory membranes moist.
- OTC options: Oral decongestants (such as pseudoephedrine or phenylephrine) reduce mucosal swelling but can elevate heart rate and blood pressure; people with hypertension should use caution. Topical nasal sprays (like oxymetazoline) should be limited to 3 consecutive days to avoid rebound congestion (rhinitis medicamentosa).

## Cough and Throat Irritation
Coughs protect the airway by expelling irritants and secretions:
- Soothing remedies: Warm tea with honey (for individuals over 1 year of age) coats the throat and significantly suppresses nighttime coughs. Warm saltwater gargles (half a teaspoon of salt in warm water) relieve localized pharyngeal pain.
- OTC choices: Dextromethorphan can help quiet dry, hacking coughs that disturb sleep. Guaifenesin (an expectorant) helps thin bronchial secretions to make productive coughs more efficient. Throat lozenges containing pectin or menthol provide temporary soothing.

## When to Seek Medical Attention for Respiratory Illness
Seek clinical guidance if a sore throat is severe and accompanied by high fever without a cough (possible streptococcal infection), if symptoms improve but then abruptly worsen with high fever and productive colored phlegm, or if shortness of breath, wheezing, or chest pain develops.
