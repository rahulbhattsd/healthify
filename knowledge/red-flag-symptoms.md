# Recognizing Emergency Symptoms and Red Flags

Health concerns often fall on a spectrum from self-limiting illnesses to life-threatening emergencies. Knowing the "red flag" warning signs helps individuals differentiate conditions suitable for home care from those requiring emergency medical services (such as calling 911 or visiting an emergency department).

## Cardiovascular and Thoracic Emergencies
Chest discomfort is never something to ignore or self-treat. Immediate emergency evaluation is required if any of the following occur:
- Sudden, crushing, squeezing, or heavy chest pain or pressure.
- Pain radiating to the jaw, neck, back, shoulders, or down one or both arms.
- Chest symptoms accompanied by cold sweats, dizziness, nausea, or profound shortness of breath.
- Palpitations associated with near-fainting (presyncope) or true syncope (loss of consciousness).

## Neurological Red Flags
Sudden deficits in central nervous system function indicate potential vascular events, such as strokes or intracranial hemorrhages. Use the FAST criteria and seek immediate care:
- Sudden facial drooping or asymmetrical smiling.
- Sudden arm or leg weakness, numbness, or loss of motor coordination on one side of the body.
- Sudden slurred speech, garbled words, or inability to comprehend spoken language.
- Sudden onset of the "worst headache of your life" (thunderclap headache).
- Sudden vision loss or diplopia (double vision).
- Acute confusion, delirium, or unresponsiveness.

## Severe Respiratory and Allergic Distress
Respiratory compromise requires rapid emergency stabilization:
- Severe difficulty breathing: gasping, inability to speak in full sentences, or blue/gray discoloration of the lips, face, or nail beds (cyanosis).
- Stridor (harsh high-pitched breathing during inspiration) indicating upper airway obstruction.
- Signs of anaphylaxis: sudden hives or facial swelling accompanied by throat tightness, wheezing, dizziness, or vomiting following exposure to an allergen.

## Acute Abdominal and Systemic Warnings
- Severe, unbearable abdominal pain that worsens with movement or is rigid to the touch.
- Persistent vomiting with blood (hematemesis) or bowel movements that appear black/tarry (melena) or contain bright red blood.
- High persistent fever combined with a stiff neck and sensitivity to light (photophobia), suggestive of meningitis.
