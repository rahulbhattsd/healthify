# Understanding and Managing Fevers

A fever is a temporary elevation in normal body temperature, typically defined as an oral temperature of 100.4°F (38.0°C) or higher. In most cases, a fever represents a natural and healthy physiological defense mechanism initiated by the immune system to fight off viral or bacterial infections.

## Causes and Common Characteristics
Fevers commonly accompany upper respiratory viral infections, influenza, gastrointestinal bugs, or localized inflammatory reactions. Mild to moderate fevers help slow the replication of pathogens while stimulating white blood cell production. Accompanying symptoms often include chills, sweating, muscle aches, mild headaches, and generalized fatigue.

## Home Care and Hydration
Most uncomplicated fevers in adults can be managed comfortably at home. Key measures include:
- Adequate fluid intake: Drink plenty of water, electrolyte solutions, clear broths, or herbal teas to replace fluid lost through perspiration.
- Rest: Conserve physical energy to support the immune response.
- Environmental cooling: Wear lightweight, breathable clothing and keep the room at a comfortable, moderate temperature. Avoid heavy blankets or cold baths, which can induce shivering and raise internal core temperatures.

## Over-the-Counter Management
Fever reducers (antipyretics) such as acetaminophen or ibuprofen can help alleviate discomfort and lower high temperatures. These medications do not cure the underlying infection but improve comfort. Always follow package dosage limits and verify safety with pre-existing liver, kidney, or gastrointestinal conditions.

## When to Seek Immediate Medical Evaluation
Seek medical care if a fever persists beyond three days, exceeds 103°F (39.4°C) in adults, or fails to respond to antipyretics. Red flags requiring emergency attention include fever accompanied by a stiff neck, sudden confusion, difficulty breathing, an unexplained rash, or persistent vomiting that prevents fluid retention.
